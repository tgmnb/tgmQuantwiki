## 摘要

Recent advances in deep reinforcement learning (RL) have shown that improving neural network architectures can yield substantial gains in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance without altering the underlying algorithms. In contrast, work on multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL), which aims to discover a set of policies that balance trade-offs among conflicting objectives, has predominantly focused on algorithmic innovations, leaving the area of architectures underexplored.
